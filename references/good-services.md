## Original
The 15 principles, titles word for word from the site:
1. Be easy to find
2. Clearly explain the purpose of your service
3. Set the expectations a user has of your service
4. Enable each user to complete the outcome they set out to do
5. Work in a way that is familiar
6. Require no prior knowledge to use
7. Be agnostic of organisational structures
8. Require the minimum possible steps to complete
9. Be consistent throughout
10. Have no dead ends
11. Be usable by everyone, equally
12. Encourage the right behaviours from users and service providers
13. Respond to change quickly
14. Clearly explain why a decision has been made
15. Make it easy to get human assistance

Principle 10, in full: "A service should direct all users to a clear outcome, regardless of whether the user is eligible or suitable to use the service. No user should be left behind or stranded within a service without knowing how to continue."

Principle 14, in full: "When a decision is made within a service, it should be obvious to a user why this decision has been made and clearly communicated at the point at which it's made. A user should also be given a route to contest this if they need to."

Principle 4 opens: "A good service helps the user to achieve a goal – be that start a business, learn to drive or move house…"

The Good Services Scale, word for word:
| Level | Downe's description |
|---|---|
| 5 Good | "Your service is easily findable and usable by anyone who needs to use it, enabling all users to consistently achieve the goal they set out to achieve." |
| 4 Moderate | "Most users are able to find and use your service successfully, although some users may still be excluded." |
| 3 Unsatisfactory | "Though some users will be able to use your service to reach their desired goal, the service is still difficult to use for the majority of users." |
| 2 Poor | "Though a minority of users might be able to complete your service it is unlikely to get them to the goal they need to achieve." |
| 1 Bad | "Only the most expert and determined users can find and use this service, it is impossible for most." |

## For and not for
The scale page says it is for: "Assess the quality of your service using the 15 Principles of Good Service Design". It also lists "Self-assess the quality of your service", "Benchmark your service" and "Prioritise where to invest in service improvement."
Our summary: for a whole service, end to end, across channels and actors.
Our summary: not for one screen in isolation. A screen can pass every principle while the service around it fails.

## Our operationalisation
- The service lens reads the whole journey, not only the changed screen. Read the stages before and after the change.
- Each finding names the principle by number and title, for example "Good Services 10, Have no dead ends".
- A state that leaves a person with no next action in the service is a principle 10 finding. Name the journey stage where it happens.
- A decision the person sees without its reason, or without a route to contest it, is a principle 14 finding.
- Work that the change moves to another actor, or out of sight, is a principle 7 or 8 finding. Name the actor.
- The lens may give one Good Services level (1–5) for the whole journey. That level is Downe's. The per-finding severity is ours.
- Dimensions this lens covers: outcome alignment, journey completeness, service continuity.

Cite the principle by name and number. State the evidence in the artifact. A principle with no bearing on a named decision is not cited.

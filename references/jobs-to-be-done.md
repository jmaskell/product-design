## Original
Christensen Institute: "Everyone has Jobs to Be Done in their lives—the progress they're trying to make as they strive toward a goal or aspiration within particular circumstances."
Christensen Institute: "All Jobs incorporate functional, social, and emotional forces at play in decision-making."
*Competing Against Luck* page: "Customers don't buy products or services; they 'hire' them to do a job."

Moesta and Spiek: "Two forces drive a switch: the push of the situation and the pull of a new solution. Two forces hold it back: the anxiety of the new and the habit of the present."

Strategyn: "A job is the task, goal, or objective a customer is trying to accomplish in a given situation."
Strategyn: "All jobs are made up of the same eight steps": define, locate, prepare, confirm, execute, monitor, modify, conclude.
Strategyn describes desired outcomes as "metrics customers use to measure success", "each solution-free, measurable, and stable over time."
Unverified: the outcome statement form "direction + metric + object of control + contextual clarifier" (for example "Minimise the time it takes to …"). The fetched pages do not state it.

Klement (Intercom): the job story format is "When [situation]… [motivation]… so that [outcome]."
Klement (Intercom): "Personas are imaginary customers defined by attributes that don't acknowledge causality."
Unverified: the exact wording "When …, I want to …, so I can …". The fetched page gives the three parts, not these words.

## Three formulations (keep them separate)
| | Christensen / Moesta | Ulwick (ODI) | Klement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit | Progress in a circumstance | A task, goal or objective in a situation | A situation, a motivation and an outcome |
| Form | A hire for a job; four forces of a switch | A job map of eight steps; desired outcome statements | A job story |
| Method | Switch interviews about a recent change | Outcome statements that people rate | Stories that a team writes before design |

Unverified: the Method row. The fetched pages do not describe the methods.

## For and not for
Our summary: for naming what a person tries to get done, apart from the product that serves it.
Our summary: for comparing a requested solution with other ways to reach the same outcome.
Our summary: not for one mandatory ideology. Pick the formulation that fits the question.
Our summary: not for a job that names product behaviour ("use the export button").

## Our operationalisation
- Default form: a job story ("When <situation>, I want to <motivation>, so I can <outcome>"). Use it when the trigger matters most.
- Use forces of progress (push, pull, anxiety, habit) when the question is why people switch or do not switch.
- Use an outcome statement (direction + metric + object) when you must compare solutions on one measure.
- Name the job at the person's goal level, never the product's behaviour.

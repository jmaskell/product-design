## Original elements
NN/g definition: "A journey map is a visualization of the process that a person goes through in order to accomplish a goal."

NN/g names five elements:
| Element | NN/g words |
|---|---|
| Actor | "The actor is the persona or user who experiences the journey." |
| Scenario and expectations | "The scenario describes the situation that the journey map addresses and is associated with an actor's goal or need and specific expectations." |
| Journey phases | "Journey phases are the different high-level stages in the journey." |
| Actions, mindsets and emotions | "… behaviors, thoughts, and feelings the actor has throughout the journey …" |
| Opportunities | "… insights gained from mapping; they speak to how the user experience can be optimized." |

NN/g on evidence: "Actors usually align with personas and their actions in the map are rooted in data."
NN/g on scope: "Provide one point of view per map in order to build a strong, clear narrative."
NN/g on story maps: "journey maps are meant for discovery and understanding (think big picture), while user story maps are for planning and implementation (think little picture)."
NN/g on blueprints: "If journey maps are the children to experience maps, then service blueprints are the grandchildren."

Unverified: Kalbach's term "alignment diagram" as the family name for these maps. The search result names it; I did not read his definition.

## For and not for
Our summary: for placing one change inside the whole outcome a person wants, from trigger to end, also outside the product.
Our summary: for showing which stages a change touches, and which actor acts in each stage.
Our summary: not for a record of fact when nobody did research. A map built from assumptions is fiction. Tag each cell, so the reader sees which parts are fiction.
Our summary: not for planning the build. That is a story map or a task list.

## Our operationalisation
- One journey per outcome. Other actors appear as rows when they act in a stage.
- Three levels, always in this order: experience journey (the whole outcome, also outside the product) → task flow (steps and decisions for one goal) → interface flow (screens; designing-interactions owns it).
- Every cell that states behaviour carries an evidence tag.
- Add the Backstage section only when a stage has a hand-off or work the person cannot see.

# Service Standard

## Source
- Government Digital Service. "Service Standard". GOV.UK Service Manual. Confirmed by fetching https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard and the pages for points 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 (2026-10-07).

## Original
The page opens: "The Service Standard helps teams to create and run great public services."

The 14 points, titles word for word:
1. Understand users and their needs
2. Solve a whole problem for users
3. Provide a joined up experience across all channels
4. Make the service simple to use
5. Make sure everyone can use the service
6. Have a multidisciplinary team
7. Use agile ways of working
8. Iterate and improve frequently
9. Create a secure service which protects users' privacy
10. Define what success looks like and publish performance data
11. Choose the right tools and technology
12. Make new source code open
13. Use and contribute to open standards, common components and patterns
14. Operate a reliable service

The summary under each point this lens uses, word for word:
- Point 1: "Develop a deep understanding of users and the problem you're trying to solve for them."
- Point 2: "Work towards creating a service that solves a whole problem for users, working with other teams and organisations where necessary. Design and develop your service around the needs of users, not around technologies or pre-selected solutions."
- Point 3: "Work towards creating a service that meets users' needs across all the channels they need. This could be a mix of online, phone, paper and face to face."
- Point 4: "Build a service that's simple, intuitive and comprehensible. And test it with users to make sure it works for them."
- Point 5: "Provide a service that everyone can use, including disabled people and people with other legally protected characteristics. And people who do not have access to the internet or lack the skills or confidence to use it."
- Point 10: "Work out what success looks like for your service and identify metrics which will tell you what's working and what can be improved, combined with user research."

"Solve a whole problem" guidance (point 2, "Why it's important"):
- "Services that do not work well with other related services make it hard for users to do what they need to."
- "However we should be careful not to build big, complicated services that are not intuitive to use because they try to do too much."
- "And it does not mean trying to fix everything at once. Making incremental and frequent improvements benefits users."

Point 2, "What it means", selected bullets word for word:
- "make sure services are scoped according to how users think and what they need to do - not too narrow or too broad"
- "be able to explain how the service the team is working on will join up with other things into a journey that solves a whole problem for users"
- "consider alternatives to creating a service - for example publishing website content, running a campaign, partnering with a non-government organisation or making data or an Application Programming Interface (API) available to third parties"
- "work towards minimising the number of times users have to provide the same information to government (while respecting users' privacy), for example by using a single sign-on"

## For and not for
Our summary: for public services built by government teams. The Standard is the bar a service meets in assessment.
Our summary: not for design critique in points 6–9 and 11–14. Those points are about teams and delivery. This lens does not use them.

## Our operationalisation
- The service lens uses points 1–5 and 10 only.
- Point 2 is the main test: does the change solve the whole problem the person has, or only the part on one screen?
- Point 3 catches a step that works online and breaks on the phone, by email or on paper.
- Point 10 asks whether the issue names how the team will know the change worked. A missing measure is a MINOR or UNKNOWN finding, not a design failure.
- "Government", "public" and "department" in the original stand for the organisation that runs the service.
- Dimensions this lens covers: outcome alignment, journey completeness, service continuity.

# Visual structure

How a screen is ordered and grouped so people find what they came for. Structure only: type, colour and spacing values belong to the project's visual-design tooling.

## Source
- Johnson, J. *Designing with the Mind in Mind*. 3rd ed. Morgan Kaufmann, 2020. Ch. 2 (pp. 15–17, Gestalt proximity and common region), ch. 6 (pp. 94–96, scanning), ch. 7 (p. 115, one call to action).
- Cooper, A., Reimann, R., Cronin, D. and Noessel, C. *About Face*. 4th ed. Wiley, 2014. Ch. 17 (pp. 410–414, information hierarchy and grouping).

## Authors' positions (our words)
- Johnson: things that sit close together read as a group, whatever they look like. People scan for what serves their goal and skip the rest. A screen asking for action should have one dominant action per goal.
- Cooper: rank each item as needed at once, sometimes, or only by exception. Show the rank with position, size and contrast. When two items compete, quieten the lesser one. Group what is used together, with space before boxes.

## For and not for
For: detail pages, lists and tables, forms and any screen with mixed-importance content. In build work and in design.
Not for: which control to use or where an action sits (references/controls-and-dialogs.md); visual styling.

## Order and grouping
- Order information in the sequence the person uses it: what they check first comes first.
- When a page holds a long run of facts (about 8 or more), group them under short sub-headings in that order. A flat list makes people search; groups let them skip.
- Put related items closer to each other than to anything else. Use space before borders and boxes.
- Lead each heading, label and row with the word people scan for.

## Lists are for choosing
- A list exists so people can find and choose an item. A column earns its place only if people sort, scan or choose by it.
- Put the column that decides the next action nearest the item's name.
- A fact people filter by gets its own column, not a tag under the name.
- Flags and secondary facts go on the item's page, as an alert when someone must act on them (references/needed-now.md).
- The same kind of list looks the same everywhere in the product, at full width.

## One primary action
- Each screen has one primary action per goal. Every other action is quieter.
- Once the person has committed to a goal (paying, applying), remove links and offers that lead away from it.
- Rank every element by when it's needed: at once, sometimes, or by exception. Show the rank with position and prominence. Quieten the lesser of two competing items rather than making the greater one louder.
